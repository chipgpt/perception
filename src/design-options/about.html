How sharp is your perception?

Five quick puzzles. Five different ways to get surprisingly close—or spectacularly wrong.

Perception is a free daily browser game combining visual puzzles, timing, memory, and trivia. Explore an answer space: rotate a 3D shape, pick a color on a wheel, slide through a timeline, or turn a duration dial. Make your guess, then see how close you came.

One daily challenge. 500 possible points.

  1. Open the game and jump straight into Daily Five.
  2. Play five different puzzle types. Every round is worth up to 100 points.
  3. See your guess and the target together, with an explanation after each answer.
  4. Finish your five rounds and share your results to compare with friends.

How scoring works

Time uses linear scoring in daily challenges from October 16, 2026, and in new practice rounds. Its zero-point allowance is the greater of 1.25 seconds or 50% of the target duration. Only an exact match earns full accuracy; the minimum allowance is not a free-error window. Earlier daily rounds and saved results keep their original scoring.

When? uses linear scoring in daily challenges from October 16, 2026, and in new practice rounds. Its zero-point allowance is the greater of five years or 25% of the event’s age, measured in the challenge year. Within the five-year allowance, each year missed costs 20 points: a four-year miss earns 20. Earlier daily rounds and saved results retain their original scoring.

All new practice rounds and daily challenges from October 16, 2026 use linear scoring. Each game measures error against its own zero-point bound, then awards 100 times the remaining closeness. Using 10% of that allowance earns 90 points; 25% earns 75; half earns 50. An exact match earns 100, and reaching the bound earns zero.

For the new linear scoring version, 3D view reaches zero points at 90° of rotation error; Angles reaches zero at 180°. A 45° miss earns 50 points in 3D view, while a 90° miss earns 50 in Angles.

Size it up scores width and height independently, each worth up to 50 points, then rounds the combined score. A 5% miss in both dimensions earns 90/100. A 50% error in one dimension earns zero for that dimension. Earlier daily rounds and saved questions retain their original scoring.

Perspective measures your miss against the inner box: 40% of its diagonal earns zero. How long? reaches zero at 50% shorter or longer than the target; a 25% miss earns 50 points. From October 17 (and in new practice rounds), Line memory starts with the percentage of the target stroke covered. Painted area up to 1.25 times the target’s area has no penalty; each extra 1% of target area beyond that deducts one point. October 16 and saved overlap-scoring rounds keep their original shared-area ÷ combined-area rule. These rules apply to the new scoring version.

The game’s “How scoring works” instructions list the bounds for each puzzle. Your results show small comparisons so you can see where you were close.

A little more practice?

Open the Practice menu to pick an individual game or a fresh mixed five. Daily Five is the only daily challenge; individual games are practice-only. Trivia practice uses questions from previous Daily Fives, keeping today’s, upcoming, and unused trivia out of the practice pool. As the archive grows, sessions offer up to five different past questions.

Good to know

Is Perception free? Do I need an account?

It is free to play, with no account or login required.

Can I play on my phone?

Yes. The controls support touch and desktop play. Every game is silent, including rhythm—there is no audio to enable.

When does the daily challenge change?

The date on your device determines the daily challenge. Open or restart Daily Five after your calendar date changes to get the new day’s puzzles.

Where are my results saved?

Daily progress, history, and your anonymous player ID are stored in your browser. Clearing site data resets them. They do not sync between devices. Minimal Umami analytics measure daily visits, starts, completions with scores, and one share-button click per browser per day.

Does sharing reveal the answers?

The shared results contain the date, puzzle emojis, individual scores, total score, and game address. They do not include the questions or their answers.
