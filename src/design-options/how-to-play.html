How points work

Each round is worth 0–100 points. Each game has an error allowance: the miss that earns zero. Points fall evenly as you use that allowance.

    or beyond

For example: in Angles, the allowance is 180°. A 45° miss uses a quarter of it, so you earn 75 points.

A smaller error number doesn’t always mean more points. Degrees, years, wheel distance, and split percentages measure different things. Compare your miss with that game’s allowance. For example, 5.5° off in 3D view earns 94 points; a 3.6 percentage-point split error in Half & Half earns 82.

Scores are rounded to whole points. Size it up averages width and height accuracy; Line memory awards the percentage of stroke area that overlaps. Their rules are below.

These rules apply to new practice rounds and Daily Fives dated October 16, 2026 onward. Earlier challenges and saved rounds keep their original scoring.

The games & their allowances

Open a game for the controls, scoring rule, and an example.

3D view 90° rotation

Study the view for three seconds. Drag the shape to turn it, then use the outer handle to tilt it back to the view you remember.

We measure the smallest 3D rotation from your view to the target. 90° off earns zero; 45° off earns 50. Errors across the three rotation axes combine into one rotation distance.

Half & Half 20 percentage points

Drag the cut or its handles to split the total filled area equally. All shapes count together; empty space doesn’t count.

50/50 earns 100; 60/40 earns 50; 70/30 or worse earns zero. A 53.6/46.4 split is 3.6 percentage points from even and earns 82 points.

Line memory Stroke overlap

Study the line for three seconds, then trace it in one stroke. Your line and the target have the same thickness. Clear to try drawing again before you lock in.

Your score is the area shared by both strokes divided by the total area covered by either stroke. 75% overlap earns 75 points; no overlap earns zero. Missing sections and extra scribbles lower your score. Speed and direction don’t matter. On reveal, a dashed center marks the target.

Perspective 40% of the inner diagonal

Tap where the receding lines would meet: the vanishing point.

Your miss is the straight-line distance from your point to the answer, measured against the inner box’s corner-to-corner diagonal. A miss of 40% of that diagonal earns zero; 20% earns 50.

Size it up 50% error per dimension

Start with one square. Drag the corner to make the requested number of squares wide and tall. The shaded starting square stays fixed as your reference. The opposite corner stays anchored.

Width and height each contribute up to 50 points. A dimension that’s 50% too big or small earns zero for that dimension. 5% off in both dimensions earns 90 points. One exact dimension and one 50% off earns 50. A wide guess can’t cancel a short one. The reveal overlays the correct grid on your dashed outline.

Rhythm ⅔× to 1½× the tempo

Watch five silent flashes, then tap five beats at the same pace. Your tap intervals determine your tempo.

Zero points at two-thirds or one-and-a-half times the target tempo. We compare proportional tempo changes, so reciprocal changes get equal scores. For a target of 100 BPM, about 82 or 122 BPM earns 50 points; 66⅔ or 150 BPM earns zero.

Angles 180°

Turn the line to the requested angle. Measure counterclockwise from the right-facing baseline.

We use the shortest angular distance. 45° off earns 75; 90° off earns 50; 180° off earns zero.

Color memory One wheel radius

Remember the swatch for two seconds, then choose its color on the wheel. Hue goes around the wheel; vividness increases toward the edge.

We measure straight-line distance between your pick and the target on the wheel. Half a wheel radius away earns 50 points; one radius or more earns zero. This measures wheel position, rather than differences between hex codes.

Time 50% of the target, minimum 1.25 s

Press Start, wait for the requested time, then Stop. There’s no clock until the reveal.

The zero-point miss is half the target duration or 1.25 seconds, whichever is larger. For a 10-second target, 2.5 seconds early or late earns 50; 5 seconds off earns zero. For a 2-second target, the allowance is 1.25 seconds.

When? 25% of its age, minimum 5 years

Scroll the year wheel to put your answer in the center. Lock it in to see the answer and source.

The zero-point miss is one-quarter of the event’s age or five years, whichever is larger. For a recent event, four years off still earns 20 points. For an event 100 years old, the allowance is 25 years. Age is fixed to the challenge year, so an old challenge’s scoring stays the same.

How long? 50% of the answer

Turn the duration dial to your best estimate. Use the unit controls and +/− buttons to refine it, then lock in to see the answer and source.

A guess 50% shorter or longer earns zero. For a two-hour answer, 90 minutes or 2½ hours earns 50; one hour or three hours earns zero. We score the duration shown by the controls.

Touch, mouse & keyboard

Drag, tap, or click the game controls. Focus a control to use the keyboard; arrow keys adjust most answers. Size it up uses left/right for width and up/down for height, with Shift for larger steps. For Line memory, arrows move the cursor and Space adds a point. Color memory also has hue and saturation sliders.

Memory games are silent. Switching away resets an active memory preview or timer.

Playing an older challenge?

Daily Fives through October 15, 2026 and previously saved questions keep their original rules and earned scores. Their points fall more steeply: we square the remaining closeness. Using half the allowance earns 25 points instead of 50. That’s why a 3.6 percentage-point miss in an older Half & Half round earns 67 points; under the current rule it earns 82.

Older allowances: 3D view 180°; Line memory average path distance of 20% of the full board diagonal; Perspective 40% of the full board diagonal; Time 3 seconds; When? 50 years; How long? one-quarter to four times the answer, measured proportionally. Angles, Half & Half, Color memory, and Rhythm use the allowances listed above with the older curve.

Older Proportions rounds ask for a ratio, independent of rectangle size, with zero at two-thirds or 1½ times the target ratio. Older Size it up rounds square width and height closeness separately, then average them. These versions remain available when replaying their challenges.
